The Texas Government Trailblazer Trek

Eighteen interactive chapters covering the full GOVT 2306 course. Free for every student, openly licensed for every instructor, and built to be adopted in pieces or whole.

Most Texas Government textbooks cost students somewhere between $60 and $130. The Trek costs nothing, requires no access code, no account, and no download. Students open a chapter in a browser and start working. Everything below is already built and in use.

Why this one is different

It is aligned to the ACGM, and the alignment is published

Every chapter is mapped to the THECB learning outcomes for GOVT 2306, to the Core Objectives, and to the statutory content requirements in Education Code §51.301 and the SB 1 civil-rights content. The matrix is public, so a curriculum committee can check the claim instead of taking anyone's word for it.

Students actually do something

Each chapter is interactive rather than a PDF. Students reveal content, answer knowledge checks, and write reflections in their own words. Each chapter generates a Completion Report recording points by section, knowledge-check accuracy, the full text of every reflection, and time on task, with a tamper-evident integrity hash. That report is a usable graded artifact and works as direct evidence for Critical Thinking and Communication assessment.

Contested topics are taught, not avoided

Abortion, voting law, immigration, affirmative action, religious displays in classrooms, and sex and gender all appear. Each is presented with the strongest argument on every side, in parallel, with no editorializing and current legal status stated plainly. The lecture slides do the same, and the speaker notes tell instructors not to share their own view. Students are asked which level of scrutiny applies, not which team is right.

Accessible, and tested rather than assumed

Every scored activity is operable by keyboard and exposed to assistive technology. Reveal cards and quiz options carry proper roles and states, feedback is announced through live regions, focus indicators are visible, and every image has descriptive alternative text. This is verified by an automated test that simulates a keyboard-only student earning points, not by inspection. Known limitations are documented openly.

It is current, and it is maintained

Texas law changes every biennium and the content is reviewed on that cadence. The modules already reflect recent developments including the 2025 legislative session, the 2025 constitutional amendments, and current officeholders. Where litigation is live, the slides tell the instructor to verify status before teaching.

You do not have to adopt all of it

Try one chapter

Assign a single module as a supplement alongside whatever you use now. Costs you one class period of setup and costs students nothing.

Use the ancillaries

Take the slide decks and the test bank even if you keep your current text. Both are editable and separately licensed for reuse.

Go all in

Replace the textbook entirely. Your students pay nothing on day one, and every one of them has the material before the first class meeting.

What it does not do yet

Any colleague evaluating a resource deserves the limits along with the pitch:

  • There is no printed edition. Everything is browser-based, which suits students with phones and inconveniences students who want paper.
  • Screen-reader testing so far is automated. Verification with JAWS, NVDA, and VoiceOver by an actual screen-reader user is still outstanding.
  • Slide decks and instructor materials are still being built out chapter by chapter.
  • Because Texas law changes every session, some content will always be a session behind until the next review pass.
